1. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW & SCOPE
Urban flooding presents high-velocity risks due to impervious concrete surfaces, undersized stormwater drainage, and rapid localized inundation. These guidelines define the AI-assisted response matrix, escalation triggers, rescue priorities, and multi-agency resource allocation rules for citywide flood incidents.

2. SEVERITY CLASSIFICATION MATRIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Level 1 - Minor Waterlogging (Advisory Alert)
- Water Depth: 5 cm to 15 cm (curb level)
- Impact: Slow vehicular traffic, minor pedestrian inconvenience.
- Action: Automated pump activation at identified low-points; municipal drain maintenance dispatch.

Level 2 - Moderate Street Inundation (Warning Alert)
- Water Depth: 16 cm to 45 cm (above curb level, entering basements)
- Impact: Stalled light motor vehicles, electrical transformer submergence risk, low-lying ground floor flooding.
- Action: Traffic diversions via smart VMS (Variable Message Signs); deployment of mobile high-capacity dewatering diesel pumps (2000+ GPM); alert local disaster response units.

Level 3 - Severe Urban Flooding (Emergency Response)
- Water Depth: 46 cm to 100 cm (waist level)
- Impact: Stranded buses/cars, structural water ingress, power grid tripping, subterranean transit disruption.
- Action: Immediate shutdown of low-lying power distribution transformers to prevent electrocution; deployment of inflatable rescue boats (IRBs) and State Disaster Response Force (SDRF); mandatory evacuation of basements and ground-floor tenements.

Level 4 - Catastrophic Inundation / Flash Flood Surge (Crisis Command)
- Water Depth: > 100 cm or rapid flow velocity exceeding 1.5 m/s
- Impact: Structural collapse threats, vehicles washed away, widespread isolation of residential pockets.
- Action: National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) / Military air-lift activation; citywide red alert; emergency sirens; total transit stoppage in affected quadrants.

3. SENSOR & TELEMETRY TRIGGER THRESHOLDS
- Ultrasonic Flood Sensor Thresholds:
  * Alert: Level rising > 5 cm in 10 minutes.
  * Alarm: Level reaching 30 cm above baseline sensor datum.
- Automated Weather Station (AWS) Rainfall Telemetry:
  * Moderate: 15-30 mm/hr
  * Heavy: 30-65 mm/hr
  * Very Heavy / Cloudburst: > 65 mm/hr
- Underpass Laser Depth Detectors:
  * Automated barrier drop activated when water depth reaches 25 cm.

4. STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES (SOP)
Phase 1: Detection & AI Verification (0 - 5 Minutes)
1. Ingest telemetry from automated water level sensors, stormwater CCTV streams, and 911 citizen calls.
2. Cross-verify incident location with topographical elevation maps and drainage GIS layers.
3. Compute inundation projection and rate of rise using hydrological prediction models.

Phase 2: Immediate Containment & Traffic Diversion (5 - 15 Minutes)
1. Automatically signal Traffic Command Center to trigger green corridors for emergency services.
2. Route civilian navigation systems away from compromised corridors, underpasses, and riverfront roads.
3. Deploy police interceptor units to physically barricade flooded underpasses.

Phase 3: Search, Rescue & Evacuation (15 - 45 Minutes)
1. Deploy Water Rescue Squadrons equipped with outboard motor boats, life jackets, and thermal night-vision gear.
2. Prioritize evacuation in this order:
   a. Submerged schools, daycare facilities, and geriatric centers.
   b. Underground transit stations and subterranean commercial basements.
   c. Slum settlements along riverbank embankments and drainage nullahs.
   d. Stranded public transit passengers.

Phase 4: Dewatering & Hazard Mitigation (Continuous)
1. Position mobile trailer-mounted high-flow sludge pumps.
2. Direct pumping discharge to designated retention ponds or deep stormwater trunks.
3. Coordinate with Electricity Distribution Company (DISCOM) for zone-by-zone isolation.

5. RESOURCE ALLOCATION & ASSET DISPATCH MATRIX
- Rescue Teams:
  * 1 Water Rescue Unit per 25 stranded individuals in Level 3 zones.
  * 2 Inflatable rescue boats (IRB) with certified swiftwater rescue technicians per square kilometer of inundation.
- Dewatering Equipment:
  * High-head submersible pumps: 4 units per flooded arterial junction.
  * Heavy-duty suction tankers: 6 units per flooded underpass.
- Sandbagging & Floodwall Barriers:
  * Rapid automated sandbag deployment trucks dispatched to hospital perimeters and critical substations.
- Medical & Relief:
  * Mobile First Aid Stations stationed at elevated staging areas.
  * Clean drinking water distribution tankers and dry ration packs dispatched to designated relief shelters.

6. DESIGNATED EVACUATION SHELTERS & CORRIDORS
- Shelters: Multi-story municipal schools, sports stadiums, and convention halls situated above 100-year flood lines.
- Safe Corridors: Elevated flyovers and arterial ring roads designated as non-flooding transit arteries.
- Animal Rescue: Livestock and domestic pets to be directed to elevated fairgrounds.

7. POST-FLOOD SANITATION & EPIDEMIC PREVENTION
- Chlorine dosing of municipal drinking water mains.
- Anti-larval spraying to prevent vector-borne disease outbreaks (Dengue, Malaria).
- Bleaching powder dispersion on cleared road surfaces.
